1. Inventory Turnover Ratio
Formula

Inventory Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold ÷ Average Inventory

Interpretation

The Inventory Turnover Ratio marginally improved from 14.52x in FY24 to 14.55x in FY25, indicating that the company continues to manage inventory efficiently despite a significant increase in business operations. Revenue from operations increased from ₹1,33,457 lakh to ₹1,47,542 lakh, while inventory increased only moderately from ₹6,240 lakh to ₹6,777 lakh. This suggests that inventory growth has remained proportionate to sales growth, reducing the risk of excess stock accumulation. From a lender's perspective, efficient inventory management supports the company's working capital cycle and indicates that inventory is being converted into sales at a healthy pace. This is a positive factor when evaluating a working capital facility.

2. Receivables Turnover Ratio
Formula

Receivables Turnover = Revenue ÷ Average Trade Receivables

Interpretation

The Receivables Turnover Ratio improved from 5.43x in FY24 to 6.16x in FY25, indicating stronger collection efficiency. Although revenue increased by approximately 10.6%, trade receivables decreased from ₹24,582 lakh to ₹23,967 lakh, demonstrating that the company collected receivables more effectively despite higher sales. Faster collections improve operating cash flow and reduce dependence on external working capital borrowings. For a Credit Analyst, this indicates lower credit risk, as timely realization of receivables enhances the company's ability to meet short-term obligations and service bank debt.

3. Quick Ratio
Formula

Quick Ratio = (Current Assets − Inventory) ÷ Current Liabilities

Interpretation

The Quick Ratio declined from 0.88x in FY24 to 0.67x in FY25, indicating a reduction in immediately available liquid assets relative to short-term liabilities. This decline was primarily driven by a reduction in cash balances and investments, along with an increase in short-term borrowings. While liquidity has weakened compared to the previous year, this movement should be viewed alongside the company's strong revenue growth and expansion activities. The lower quick ratio suggests greater reliance on inventory and operating cash flows to meet current liabilities. Consequently, the proposed working capital facility could provide additional liquidity support for day-to-day operations.

4. Current Ratio
Formula

Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

Interpretation

The Current Ratio declined from 1.00x in FY24 to 0.79x in FY25, reflecting an increase in current liabilities relative to current assets. Current borrowings and trade payables increased during the year, while current assets remained broadly stable. Although a ratio below 1.0 typically indicates tighter short-term liquidity, the company's strong operating performance, improving receivable collections, and higher revenue growth reduce immediate concerns. From a credit perspective, this trend supports the company's requirement for additional working capital financing to sustain business expansion and maintain operational liquidity

5. Payables Turnover Ratio
Formula

Payables Turnover = Purchases ÷ Average Trade Payables

Interpretation

The Payables Turnover Ratio declined from 5.19x in FY24 to 4.86x in FY25, indicating that the company is taking slightly longer to settle supplier obligations. Trade payables increased during the year, which may reflect improved supplier credit terms or a conscious working capital management strategy rather than financial stress. In the context of higher business volumes and revenue growth, extended supplier credit can support operating cash flows and reduce immediate funding pressure. However, continued monitoring is advisable to ensure that payment delays do not affect supplier relationships or operational continuity


**Overall Credit Analyst Recommendation**

Lumax Auto Technologies Limited demonstrated healthy operational performance during FY2024-25, supported by higher revenue, improved receivable collections, and efficient inventory management. Although liquidity ratios (Current Ratio and Quick Ratio) weakened due to increased short-term liabilities and business expansion, the company continues to generate strong operating cash flows and maintains a growing business with established OEM relationships. The increased working capital requirement appears to be growth-driven rather than distress-driven. Therefore, from a credit underwriting perspective, the proposed ₹5 crore Working Capital Cash Credit facility may be considered, subject to satisfactory security creation, periodic stock and debtor statements, and compliance with the bank's standard lending covenants.
